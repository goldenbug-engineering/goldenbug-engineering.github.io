Ribbit Rush Privacy Policy

Effective: 2 November 2026

Ribbit Rush is a game about a frog climbing as high as it can. This policy explains the small amount of information the game collects about how it is played, why, and how to switch it off.

The short version

  • The game sends anonymous play statistics: things like which mode you played, how high you climbed and what ended the run.
  • They are tied to a random number made by the game on your device, not to you. We never ask for your name, email, account, location, contacts or advertising ID.
  • You can switch it off at any time: Settings → Share anonymous stats. Nothing more is sent after that.
  • There are no ads and no in-app purchases, and nothing is sold or shared for advertising.

What we collect

While Share anonymous stats is on, the game records these events:

WhenWhat is recorded
The game opensThat it opened, and whether your phone's own settings have vibration or touch vibration switched off (so we can tell when haptics are silenced by the phone rather than the game).
A run startsThe mode (classic or daily) and your best score in that mode.
A run endsThe mode, the score, whether it was a new best, what ended it (for example a fall or a snake), whether you gave up, how long it lasted, how many tongue shots and grapples you made, and how smoothly the game ran (frames per second).
The wardrobeThat it was opened, and which hat, face paint or sparkle trail you chose.
SettingsChanges to haptics and volume settings.

Each event also carries:

  • A random install ID. The game makes it the first time it needs it. It is not derived from your device, your account or anything about you; it only lets us tell that two runs came from the same installation. Uninstalling the game deletes it.
  • Basic technical details: the game's version, the operating system and its version, and the device model, so we can see whether the game runs well on the phones people use.
  • Your country, as set in your phone's language and region settings (for example "GB"), so we know where the game is played. It is not worked out from your location or your connection.
  • The time of the event.

What we do not collect

No name, email address, account, phone number, precise or approximate location, contacts, photos, advertising ID, or any content from your device. The analytics service is set not to store your IP address, and the game tells it not to work out a location from your connection.

Why

To make the game better: to see which parts are too hard, what ends runs, which modes and cosmetics people enjoy, whether players come back, and whether the game runs smoothly on their phones. The data is only ever looked at in aggregate. We rely on our legitimate interest in improving the game, and you can object at any time by switching sharing off.

Who processes it

The statistics are sent to PostHog, an analytics service, which stores them on our behalf in its EU data centre. PostHog processes the data only to provide the service to us. We do not sell the data or share it with anyone else.

How long it is kept

Events are kept for up to one year, then deleted.

Switching it off

Open Settings in the game and untick Share anonymous stats. The game stops sending immediately and discards anything it had not yet sent. You can switch it back on whenever you like.

Because the data is tied only to a random ID that we cannot link to you, we cannot look up or delete an individual's data on request. If you have a question about it, contact us below.

Children

The game does not ask anyone for personal information, including children, and the statistics above contain none.

Changes

If what the game collects changes, this page will be updated and its effective date moved.
